## 2026-05-26 — Insurance-specific roadmap locked

**Decision:** The insurance vertical has its own sharper three-horizons roadmap, distinct from the group-level macro roadmap.

- **H1 (Now):** Bespoke early-risk signals for underwriting — MindMap delivers behavioral correlation scores to one insurer as a secret internal pilot. No published claims. No pricing tool. Just signals that show early predictive value.
- **H2 (12–24 months):** More precise models from real claims outcomes — the pilot now has real claims data to validate against. Models improve. The insurer starts integrating outputs into underwriting decisions. Quantum ML enters here as an accelerator for pattern refinement in high-dimensional behavioral data.
- **H3 (24+ months):** The data moat becomes the platform — years of proprietary claims-linked behavioral data make the model harder to replicate and more valuable. Switching cost compounds. MindMap becomes the insurer's long-term behavioral intelligence layer.

**Why this framing matters:** The business case does not depend on quantum on day one. It depends on early signal value (H1), which creates the data flywheel that makes quantum meaningful in H2 and the moat unassailable in H3.

---

## 2026-05-26 — Quantum's role in insurance defined

**Decision:** Quantum is an H2 accelerator, not the day-one product.

- H1 does not require quantum. Classical ML on behavioral signals is sufficient to demonstrate early correlation value.
- Quantum enters in H2 because that is when enough real claims-linked data exists for more advanced pattern-separation and optimization problems (QUBO-type) to add meaningful precision.
- H3 benefits from quantum as part of the analytics stack, but the competitive moat in H3 still comes primarily from proprietary data and model refinement, not quantum alone.

**If challenged in Q&A:** "We are not claiming quantum is required on day one. We are saying it belongs in H2 because that is when enough data exists for more advanced methods to add value."

## 2026-05-26 — Cybersecurity positioned as trust layer

**Decision:** Cybersecurity is not a feature or a compliance checkbox. It is the trust layer that makes regulated insurer adoption possible.

- PQC-ready encryption protects sensitive behavioral and claims data against harvest-now-decrypt-later attacks (direct lesson from the Aurora case).
- Zero-trust architecture and differential privacy ensure no individual-level data is exposed even inside the insurer's own systems.
- Without this layer, no regulated insurer — especially under EIOPA, FCA, or Solvency II — would allow MindMap's behavioral signals into their underwriting workflow.
- The cybersecurity layer is what converts a technically interesting platform into a commercially deployable one in a regulated industry.

**One-line for the deck:** "PQC-ready security protects sensitive claims and customer data — essential for insurer adoption."
